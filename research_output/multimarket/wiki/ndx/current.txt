The '''Nasdaq-100''' is a [[stock market index]] made up of equity securities issued by 100 of the largest non-financial companies listed on the [[Nasdaq]] stock exchange. The Nasdaq-100 is often abbreviated as NDX,<ref>{{Cite web |title=NASDAQ-100 (NDX)|url=https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/index/ndx |website=[[Nasdaq]]}}</ref> NDQ, NAS100, or US100. The Nasdaq-100 index is a [[public float]] weighted/[[capitalization-weighted index]], with a maximum weight of 24% for the largest component.

Products linked to the Nasdaq-100 include [[index fund]]s ([[exchange-traded fund]]s/ETFs and [[mutual funds]]) as well as [[Derivative (finance)|derivative]]s ([[Option (finance)|options]] and [[futures contract]]s), which are available for trading in many countries with the goal of replicating the performance of the index. Also available are modified index funds; they replicate the performance of the index with modifications such as the use of [[covered call]] strategies, equal weighting, performance buffers, [[Leverage (finance)|leverage]], inclusion of only growth or value stocks, or exclusion of certain sectors, all with the goal of changing the risk/return and [[Yield (finance)|yield]]. [[Invesco QQQ]], with approximately $500 billion in assets, has the highest average daily volume of all Nasdaq-100 ETFs. In total, there is approximately $1.4 trillion in assets tracking the index.<ref name=assets/>

==History==
The Nasdaq-100 was launched on January 31, 1985, by the Nasdaq.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/10-fun-facts-about-the-nasdaq-100-index-2018-08-03-0 | title=10 Fun Facts about the Nasdaq-100 Index | publisher=[[Nasdaq]]}}</ref> It created two indices: the Nasdaq-100, which consists of [[Industrial sector|industrial]], [[technology]], [[retail]], [[telecommunication]], [[biotechnology]], [[health care]], [[transportation]], [[mass media]] and [[tertiary sector of the economy|service]] companies, and the [[Nasdaq Financial-100]], which consists of [[bank]]s, [[Insurance companies]], [[brokerage firm]]s, and [[mortgage loan]] companies.

The base price of the index was initially set at 250. In January 1994, in advance of the launch of options trading on the index by the [[Chicago Board Options Exchange]], the value of the index halved, similar to a 2-for-1 [[stock split]], to make the index value below that of the [[Nasdaq Composite]].<ref name=nasdaq100>{{Cite web | url=https://www.nasdaq.com/docs/2021/04/28/Nasdaq-100-Tracking-Innovation-Large-Cap-Growth.pdf | title=The Nasdaq-100 | work=[[Nasdaq]] | date=April 28, 2021}}</ref>

In November 1998, to make the index suitable for the basis of an exchange-traded fund, the index weights were modified away from market cap weights.<ref name=nasdaq100/>

Foreign companies were first admitted to the Nasdaq-100 in January 1998.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://cpram.com/fra/en/individual/glossary/nasdaq-100 | title=NASDAQ 100 | work=CPRAM}}</ref>

==Selection criteria==
Entry to the index is determined by the Nasdaq Index Management Committee, which follows a strict, transparent, and rules-based methodology rather than a subjective selection process. Requirements to be added to the index include having a primary listing on the Nasdaq and an average daily traded value of at least $5 million. The index components are recalculated annually after the close of trading on the third Friday in December. The number of shares outstanding and public float of each component is updated quarterly.<ref>{{Cite web |title=NASDAQ 100 Index Methodology |url=https://indexes.nasdaq.com/docs/Methodology_NDX.pdf | work=[[Nasdaq]]}}</ref>

==Related indices==
Related indices are:
* [[Nasdaq Composite]] - includes every company with a primary listing on the Nasdaq
* [[Nasdaq Financial-100]] (IXF) - includes the largest financial companies listed on the Nasdaq
* NASDAQ Q-50 (NXTQ) - includes the 50 stocks that are next eligible for inclusion in the Nasdaq-100
* NASDAQ-100 Technology Sector (NDXT) - an equal weighted index of components of the Nasdaq-100 that have an [[industry classification]] of "Technology" per the [[Industry Classification Benchmark]]
