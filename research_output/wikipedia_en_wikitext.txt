The '''Hang Seng Index''' ('''HSI''') is a market-[[Capitalization-weighted index|capitalisation-weighted]] [[stock market index]] in [[Hong Kong]] adjusted for [[free float]]. It tracks and records daily changes in the largest stock listings on the [[Stock Exchange of Hong Kong]] (SEHK) and serves as the primary indicator of overall market performance in Hong Kong. These 88 constituent companies represent about 58% of the capitalisation of the SEHK.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.hsi.com.hk/HSI-Net/static/revamp/contents/en/dl_centre/factsheets/FS_HSIe.pdf |title=AHSI and its Total Return Index |date=August 2015 |access-date=2015-09-21 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924031624/http://www.hsi.com.hk/HSI-Net/static/revamp/contents/en/dl_centre/factsheets/FS_HSIe.pdf |archive-date=2015-09-24 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

HSI was publicized on November 24, 1969, and is currently compiled and maintained by [[Hang Seng Indexes Company Limited]], which is a [[wholly owned subsidiary]] of [[Hang Seng Bank]], one of the largest banks in Hong Kong in terms of market capitalisation.<ref name="scmp2014">{{cite news |title=A new chapter for the Hang Seng Index |url=https://www.scmp.com/comment/insight-opinion/article/1567928/new-chapter-hang-seng-index |access-date=4 February 2024 |publisher=SCMP |date=7 August 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140808204528/https://www.scmp.com/comment/insight-opinion/article/1567928/new-chapter-hang-seng-index |archive-date=8 August 2014 |url-status=live }}</ref> It is responsible for compiling, publishing and managing the Hang Seng Index and a range of other stock indexes, such as ''[[Hang Seng China Enterprises Index]]'', ''Hang Seng China AH Index Series'', ''[[Hang Seng China H-Financials Index]]'', ''[[Hang Seng Composite Index Series]]'', ''[[Hang Seng China A Industry Top Index]]'', ''Hang Seng Corporate Sustainability Index Series'' and ''Hang Seng Total Return Index Series''. Hang Seng Bank, in turn, is an indirectly wholly owned subsidiary of another listed international financial institution [[HSBC Holdings plc]], owned through [[HSBC (Hong Kong)|its Hong Kong subsidiary]].{{refn|group=nb|Hang Seng Bank was a public company and a constituent of the index until January 2026, when HSBC, its majority shareholder, [[privatise]] it and took it off the SEHK.<ref>{{cite news |title=Hang Seng Bank goes private, delists on HKEX |url=https://hongkongbusiness.hk/markets-investing/news/hang-seng-bank-goes-private-delists-hkex |access-date=1 February 2026 |work=Hong Kong Business |date=27 January 2026 |language=en}}</ref>}}

==History==

[[Ho Sin Hang]], founding chairman of the Hang Seng Bank, conceived the idea of creating the Hang Seng Index as a "[[Dow Jones Industrial Average|Dow Jones Index]] for Hong Kong".<ref name="Dragon Crown">{{cite book |last1=Kwan |first1=Stanley S. K. |author-link=Stanley Kwan (banker) |title=The Dragon and the Crown |year=2009 |publisher=Hong Kong University Press| isbn=9789622099555 |pages=[https://archive.org/details/dragoncrownhongk00kwan/page/101 101]–131 |url=https://archive.org/details/dragoncrownhongk00kwan}}</ref><ref name=nytimes>{{cite news |title=Stanley Kwan, Hang Seng Index Creator, Dies at 86 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2012/01/11/business/global/stanley-kwan-hang-seng-index-creator-dies-at-86.html |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=2012-01-10}}</ref> Along with Hang Seng Director [[Lee Quo-wei]], he commissioned Hang Seng's head of Research [[Stanley Kwan (banker)|Stanley Kwan]] to create the index in 1964,<ref name="Dragon Crown" /> the index was initially used for internal reference in the Hang Seng Bank, they debuted the index on November 24, 1969.<ref name=nytimes/>

==Investing in the Hang Seng Index==
===Mutual and exchange-traded funds===
[[Index fund]]s, including [[mutual fund]]s and [[exchange-traded fund]]s (ETFs), can replicate, before fees and expenses, the performance of the index by holding the same stocks as the index in the same proportions. ETFs that replicate the performance of the index are issued by Hang Seng Investment Management ([[Tracker Fund of Hong Kong]], {{hkex|2800}}),<ref>{{cite web |title=About us {{!}} TraHK |url=https://www.trahk.com.hk/en-hk/trahk-fund/about-us/#TF_history |publisher=Hang Seng Investment Management Limited |access-date=17 August 2025 |language=en}}</ref> [[CSOP Asset Management]] ({{Hong Kong Stock Exchange|3037}}),<ref>{{cite web |title=CSOP Hang Seng Index ETF |url=https://www.csopasset.com/en/products/hk-hsi |publisher=CSOP Asset Management |access-date=17 August 2025}}</ref> and [[iShares]] ({{Hong Kong Stock Exchange|3115}}).<ref>{{cite web |title=iShares Core Hang Seng Index ETF {{!}} 3115 |url=https://www.blackrock.com/hk/en/products/284479/ishares-core-hang-seng-index-etf |publisher=BlackRock |access-date=17 August 2025 |language=en-HK}}</ref>

CSOP Asset Management offers an [[inverse ETF]] ({{Hong Kong Stock Exchange|7300}})<ref>{{cite web |title=CSOP Hang Seng Index Daily (-1x) Inverse Product |url=https://www.csopasset.com/en/products/i-hsi |publisher=CSOP Asset Management |access-date=17 August 2025}}</ref> that produces -1x daily return of the Hang Seng Index, as well as [[leveraged ETF]]s which attempt to produce 2x the daily return of either investing in ({{Hong Kong Stock Exchange|7200}})<ref>{{cite web |title=CSOP Hang Seng Index Daily (2x) Leveraged Product |url=https://www.csopasset.com/en/products/l-hsi |publisher=CSOP Asset Management |access-date=17 August 2025}}</ref> or [[Short (finance)|shorting]] ({{Hong Kong Stock Exchange|7500}})<ref>{{cite web |title=CSOP Hang Seng Index Daily (-2x) Inverse Product |url=https://www.csopasset.com/en/products/i-2xhsi |publisher=CSOP Asset Management |access-date=17 August 2025}}</ref> the index.

==Statistics==
{{More citations needed section|date=August 2015}}

When the Hang Seng Index was first published, its base of 100 points was set equivalent to the stocks' total value as of the market close on July 31, 1964. Its all-time low is 58.61 points, reached retroactively on August 31, 1967, after the base value was established but before the publication of the index. The Hang Seng passed the 10,000 point milestone for the first time in its history on December 10, 1993, and, 13 years later, passed the 20,000 point milestone on December 28, 2006. In less than 10 months, it passed the 30,000 point milestone on October 18, 2007. Its all-time high, set on January 26, 2018, was 33,223.58 points<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.hkex.com.hk/?sc_lang=en|title=HKEX|website=www.hkex.com.hk}}</ref> at closing. From October 30, 2007, through March 9, 2008, the index lost 9,426 points or approximately 30%. On September 5, it fell past the 20,000 mark the first time in almost a year to a low of 19,708.39, later closing at 19,933.28. On October 8, 2008, the index closed at 15,431.73, over 50% less than the all-time high and the lowest closing value in over two years. On October 27, 2008, the index further fell to 10,676.29 points, having fallen nearly two-thirds from its all-time peak, but passed the 20,000 point milestone again to 20,063.93 on 24 July 2009. The index reached 25,000.00 on August 19, 2014, reaching as high as 25,201.21 that day, later closing at 24,909.26 points. It continued rising to hit 26,000 on April 8, 2015, with a close of 26,236.86 The following day, it rose to as much as 27,922.67 before closing at 26,944.39. Yet again, another milestone was reached on April 13, 2015, rising to over 28,000 points, or closing to 28,016.34, the highest since December 2007. On July 8, 2015, the index fell as much as 2139 points. On 21 August the index entered a [[bear market]].
The index at that point hovered around 18000–19000 points,<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2015-08-21/hong-kong-stocks-tumble-into-bear-market-amid-global-selloff | title=Hong Kong Stocks Enter Bear Market After Falling From April Peak | publisher=Bloomberg L.P. | date=August 21, 2015 | access-date=August 23, 2015 | author=Nishizawa, Kana | author2=Haigh, Adam}}</ref> until it ended in February 2016. On 24 June 2016, the market fell 1,000 points in response to [[2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum|UK EU referendum]] results that "Leave" won the vote against "Remain". Then, the markets continuously fell on 27 and 28 June 2016 before recovered slightly on 29 June 2016.
