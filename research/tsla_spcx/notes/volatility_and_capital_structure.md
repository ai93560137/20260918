## Key Question 2 — How do desks describe relative-vol / dispersion between related single names, what does the VRP literature say for single stocks, and what are the P&L drivers and failure modes?

### Takeaway
Desks run dispersion as vega-neutral short index vol / long single-stock vol, which is effectively short implied correlation; 2026 was a boom-then-bust year for the trade (large profits, then a March correlation shock that produced the JPMorgan dispersion index's worst month since 2011, followed by near-record-low correlation by June/July and a pullback in positioning). The academic literature finds a robust negative variance/volatility risk premium at the index level, a much weaker and more heterogeneous one in single stocks, a priced correlation risk premium that explains much of the index VRP, and cross-sectional single-stock option mispricing linked to the IV–RV gap (Goyal & Saretto) and to idiosyncratic volatility/arbitrage costs (Cao & Han). Failure modes documented across sources: correlation spikes in stress, earnings/idiosyncratic jumps that are under- or over-priced, and transaction costs that erase the single-stock edge.

### Cited Findings
**Desk descriptions and 2026 market context**
- Dispersion sells index option volatility and buys constituent option volatility weighted vega-neutral; it is "effectively short implied correlation: it profits when stocks move on their own (low realized correlation) and loses when they move together" — [FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/dispersion-trading-implied-correlation-index-vs-single-stock-vol)
- Index options tend to trade rich relative to single-stock options (correlation risk premium); studies of S&P 500 options over 1996–2020 "typically find implied correlation exceeding realised by roughly 10 percentage points on average" (secondary source) — [FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/dispersion-trading-implied-correlation-index-vs-single-stock-vol)
- Dispersion "is a sort of correlation trading... loses money during stress periods when correlation rises" — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/dispersion-trading/)
- Buraschi, Trojani & Vedolin: writers of index options earn high returns due to a significant volatility risk premium, "but writers of options in single-stock markets earn lower returns" — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/dispersion-trading/)
- IFR (2026): AI-driven market shake-up fuelled a "dispersion trading craze"; UBS observed the gap between single-stock and index realised vol at a record level in the US; OptionMetrics warned dispersion trades were "meaningfully riskier" than in the past — [IFR](https://www.ifre.com/topic-codes/2386125/ais-stock-market-shakeup-fuels-dispersion-trading-craze)

**Academic literature on single-stock VRP / option returns**
- Bakshi & Kapadia (2003, RFS 16(2):527–566): delta-hedged S&P 500 option portfolios underperform zero; underperformance is less for options away from the money and greater in high-volatility periods; evidence "supportive of a negative market volatility risk premium" — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v16y2003i2p527-566.html)
- Carr & Wu (2009, RFS), "Variance Risk Premiums": model-free measure using the gap between realized variance and a synthetic variance-swap rate built from an option portfolio; a secondary source confirms they also examine individual-stock VRPs (results not captured in the extract) — [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/354970); [Netspar/Driessen](https://www.netspar.nl/wp-content/uploads/061_Driessen.pdf)
- Driessen, Maenhout & Vilkov (2009, JF 64(3):1377–1406): using S&P 100 index options and component options, find priced correlation risk; argue the correlation risk premium "cannot be exploited with realistic trading frictions" (limits-to-arbitrage) — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v64y2009i3p1377-1406.html); [Tilburg repository](https://repository.tilburguniversity.edu/items/3f5ec1dc-a815-49fd-8575-e951fa6ed057)
- Driessen, Maenhout & Vilkov (2013 WP): gap between average implied and realized correlations is "direct evidence of a large negative correlation risk premium"; the index VRP "can be attributed to the high price of correlation risk" — [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/360626)
- Decomposition: market VRP = correlation risk premium + sum of individual-stock VRPs (2020 AEA conference paper) — [AEA](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2020/preliminary/paper/dKnKSRZn)
- Goyal & Saretto (2009, JFE 94:310–326, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2009.01.001): sort stocks on the gap between historical realized vol and ATM implied vol; a zero-cost strategy long the large-positive-gap decile and short the large-negative-gap decile (straddles and delta-hedged calls/puts) earns economically and statistically significant monthly returns, robust across market conditions, risk characteristics, industries and option liquidity, not explained by standard factor models — [UPenn copy](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/CrossOptions.pdf); [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357238)
- Goyal & Saretto (2008 WP) also documented the index-vs-components IV difference via a straddle strategy (sell index options, buy component options) — [NOVA thesis citing it](https://run.unl.pt/bitstreams/e5e3892f-5eee-4bb5-bff2-11dce5130536/download)
- Cao & Han (2013, JFE 108:231–249): delta-hedged option returns fall as the underlying's idiosyncratic volatility rises; not explained by standard risk factors; explanation is dealers charging a higher premium for options on high-idiosyncratic-vol stocks due to higher arbitrage costs; controlling for limits-to-arbitrage proxies reduces the relation by about 40%; a long-low-IVOL/short-high-IVOL delta-hedged call strategy earns ~2% per month before costs, and profits disappear with realistic trading costs — [OptionMetrics summary](https://optionmetrics.com/research/j-cao-and-b-han-cross-section-of-option-returns-and-idiosyncratic-stock-volatility/); [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/356714); [PolyU record](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/cross-section-of-option-returns-and-idiosyncratic-stock-volatilit/)
- Replications of Bakshi-Kapadia outside the US give mixed results (KOSPI 200: ATM delta-hedged gains not significantly negative, OTM/puts negative) — [Emerald JDQS](https://www.emerald.com/jdqs/article/17/2/67/205260/The-Volatility-Risk-Premium-in-the-KOSPI200-Index)

### Inferences
- A TSLA/SPCX relative-vol trade is a two-name "micro-dispersion": its P&L = (realised vol spread − implied vol spread) − carry on the two gamma legs − correlation effects if structured through a basket. The key asymmetries: SPCX has non-overlapping earnings dates (Nov 3–17 vs TSLA Oct 28), staggered lockup tranches (Oct 9, Oct 24, post-Q3, Dec 8 — see KQ3) and a thin ~4% float, so SPCX realised vol is driven by supply events that TSLA does not have; TSLA has its own idiosyncratic catalysts (robotaxi/FSD, deliveries) not shared by SPCX.
- The literature's consistent message — index VRP is robust, single-stock VRP is weak/heterogeneous and partly a compensation for dealer arbitrage costs (Cao & Han), and single-stock option profits vanish after costs — implies that systematically shorting SPCX vol against TSLA vol on the IV level difference alone is not supported; Goyal-Saretto suggests conditioning on each name's own IV–RV gap instead (SPCX's gap was ~100 vol points in late July 2026 and has since largely closed).
- Failure modes specific to this pair: (1) merger announcement collapses the spread (correlation → 1, SPCX vol re-prices to deal vol); (2) lockup tranche supply shocks hit SPCX realised vol asymmetrically; (3) a Musk-specific event hits both names simultaneously (correlation spike, as in the March 2026 dispersion drawdown mechanism); (4) short-SPCX-vol legs face the "tails too dangerous to sell" problem Susquehanna flagged.

**Academic literature on IPO lending, constraints and put–call parity**
- Geczy, Musto & Reed (2002, JFE), "Stocks are special too": IPOs are "invariably on special at first, with an average specialness of more than 300 basis points", settling to mature-stock levels after insider-selling restrictions end at 180 days; most IPOs are loaned on first settlement day; IPO loans are cheap relative to documented shorting-strategy profits; underperformance around lockup expiration remains significant even for cheap-to-borrow IPOs; figures are wholesale terms, retail rebates are much smaller — [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/entities/publication/d071c291-2b0e-4dba-93b4-2432b0252006); [Knowledge@Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/equity-loans-how-to-sell-what-you-do-not-own/)
- D'Avolio (2002, JFE 66): April 2000–Sept 2001 lending data; only 9% of stocks had fees above 1% p.a.; these "specials" had a mean fee of 4.3%; a value-weighted portfolio cost ~25 bp/yr to borrow; fewer than 1% of lent stocks had negative rebates; specials and recalls rise with divergence of opinion — [Cochrane copy](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/davolio_borrowing_stock_JF.pdf); [NBER paper quoting figures](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f228879.pdf)
- Patatoukas, Sloan & Wang ("Short-Sales Constraints and Aftermarket IPO Pricing"; published as "Valuation Uncertainty and Short-Sales Constraints: Evidence from the IPO Aftermarket", Management Science 2022): most susceptible IPOs show first-day returns of 44% and lockup-expiration returns of −10%; average indicative lending fees topped 15%, utilization >75%, fails-to-deliver >20x normal, constraints peaking around lockup expiration; small offering size limits lendable shares — [MIT Sloan PDF](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Short-sales%20Constraints%20and%20Aftermarket%20IPO%20Pricing.pdf); [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/389190); [Bauer news](https://bauer.uh.edu/news/2018/bauer-research-examines-the-driving-forces-of-aftermarket-ipo-pricing)
- Lockup-expiry event studies: permanent ~40% increase in trading volume and three-day abnormal return of −1.5% at expiry; short interest larger for stocks with more downside potential at expiry — [ResearchGate (Field & Hanka)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235272518_Market_Reaction_to_the_Expiration_of_IPO_Lockup_Provisions); [ScienceDirect (short selling around lockups)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426617302339)
- Ofek, Richardson & Whitelaw (2004, JFE 74, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2003.05.008): significant put–call parity violations strongly related to the rebate-rate spread; asymmetric in the direction of short-sale constraints; survive transaction costs; stocks with negative rebate spreads show stock prices up to 7.5% above option-implied prices in the extreme 1% tail; violations and rebate spreads predict future returns (cumulative abnormal returns exceeding 65% over the 2½-year sample per NBER abstract; 70% in the NYU WP) — [NBER w9423](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9423); [NYU archive](https://archive.nyu.edu/handle/2451/26831)
- Lamont & Thaler (2003, 3Com/Palm): 3Com stub value fell to −$63 at end of first trading day; shorting costs made the trade impractical; buying puts did not work either because put–call parity was widely violated, since arbitrage between stocks and options required shorting the stock — [Cochrane NBER report](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/Cochrane_NBER_program_report_winter05.pdf); [NBER Reporter](https://www2.nber.org/reporter/winter05/lamont.html)
- Battalio & Schultz (2006, "Options and the Bubble"): using intraday options data for 1999–2000 internet stocks, find no evidence short-sale restrictions prevented shorting; synthetic shorts via puts/calls received almost as much as actual shorts — [NBER Reporter summary](https://www.nber.org/reporter/spring05/short-sale-constraints-and-overpricing)
- Engelberg, Reed & Ringgenberg (2018, JF), "Short-Selling Risk": the risks that loans become expensive or are recalled affect cross-sectional prices; stocks with more short-selling risk have lower returns, less price efficiency and less short selling; prior literature treated these as a static cost — [Wharton PDF](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/riggenberg.pdf); [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/359559)

### Inferences
- SPCX is anomalous relative to the IPO literature: utilization ~95% and short interest ~31–35% of float in August 2026, yet fee ~0.4% in late September — consistent with the huge dollar float ($75B IPO) and broad institutional lending (the "Facebook/Uber" pattern, not the "Beyond Meat/Lyft" pattern). The binding constraint is quantity (availability down from >30M to ~7M shares), not price. For a pair trade, the SPCX short leg is currently cheap to carry but exposed to the Engelberg-Reed-Ringgenberg recall/fee-spike risk precisely around the tranche dates (Oct 24, post-Q3, Dec 8) — the opposite of most IPOs, where supply arrives at expiry and loosens borrow; here supply arrives in steps while retail/insider lenders may not lend.
- Put–call parity: with fee ~0.4%, the ORW mechanism predicts negligible synthetic-vs-cash price gaps in SPCX today; the CNBC Oct 6 observation that puts carry equal-or-higher IV than calls is conventional skew, not a constraint signal. In June, when IV was 100%+ and lending was just starting, PCP deviations would have been the thing to monitor, but no source reported them.
- Shorting the SPCX leg synthetically via SSPC (-2x ETF) or via puts/risk-reversals is feasible and liquid (SSPC $2.56B first-week volume; 1.7M contracts/day in October), which is the Battalio-Schultz world rather than the Lamont-Thaler world — but the -2x ETF's daily compounding makes it unsuitable as a multi-week pair hedge.

## Key Question 5 — Idiosyncratic-volatility and lottery-stock literature (Ang-Hodrick-Xing-Zhang 2006; Bali-Cakici-Whitelaw 2011): does it suggest a direction for a TSLA/SPCX relative trade?

### Takeaway
The literature finds that high idiosyncratic-volatility and high-MAX ("lottery") stocks earn lower subsequent returns (AHXZ 2006; BCW 2011, >1%/month low-minus-high MAX spread), that the IVOL puzzle is largely absorbed by MAX, and (Cao & Han 2013) that options on high-IVOL names are priced rich (delta-hedged returns lower). Mechanically this points to "underweight the more lottery-like name" and "sell its options relative to the calmer name"; in June 2026 that was unambiguously SPCX (IV 2–4x TSLA, +50%-in-3-months tail priced at 15%), but by October 2026 SPCX's IV (50–55) is only modestly above TSLA's (~41–42) and SPCX has been the calmer stock in realised terms since August — so the literature's direction is weaker now and depends on which name screens as the lottery stock at the time of entry.

### Cited Findings
- Ang, Hodrick, Xing & Zhang (2006, JF 61(1):259–299): documented a negative relation between idiosyncratic volatility and subsequent returns, treated as a puzzle since — [IDEAS/RePEc summary via EFM 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/eufman/v25y2019i3p655-683.html); [Lincoln repository](https://researcharchive.lincoln.ac.nz/entities/publication/b03eecef-1a21-4abb-b7cb-b46f48e768bb)
- Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw (2011, JFE 99(2):427–446), "Maxing out": negative relation between prior-month maximum daily return (MAX) and expected returns; low-minus-high MAX decile raw and risk-adjusted spreads exceed 1% per month; robust to size, B/M, momentum, reversal, liquidity and skewness; including MAX reverses the AHXZ negative IVOL–return relation — [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/356984); [NBER w14804 summary](https://causalclaims.trfetzer.com/paper/w14804.html)
- Bali & Cakici (2008): equal weighting makes the IVOL puzzle vanish; later work (1962–2018) shows MAX reverses the IVOL effect, MAX+MIN removes it entirely, and the effect weakens after 1991 — [Lincoln thesis](https://researcharchive.lincoln.ac.nz/entities/publication/b03eecef-1a21-4abb-b7cb-b46f48e768bb); [JMS replication](https://jums.ub.uni-muenchen.de/JMS/article/download/5167/3450)
- Lottery-preference paper (EFM 2019): the IVOL puzzle is stronger within stocks held primarily by investors with lottery-like preferences and during downturns — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/eufman/v25y2019i3p655-683.html)
- "Have we solved the idiosyncratic volatility puzzle?" (JFE 2016) surveys competing explanations — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v121y2016i1p167-194.html)
- Cao & Han (2013): options on high-IVOL stocks have lower delta-hedged returns (richer), attributed to dealer arbitrage costs — [OptionMetrics](https://optionmetrics.com/research/j-cao-and-b-han-cross-section-of-option-returns-and-idiosyncratic-stock-volatility/)
- Patatoukas-Sloan-Wang: IPOs with high valuation uncertainty and tight lendable supply show +44% first-day returns and −10% lockup-expiration returns (Miller 1977 overvaluation mechanism) — [MIT Sloan PDF](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Short-sales%20Constraints%20and%20Aftermarket%20IPO%20Pricing.pdf)
- SPCX's retail/lottery character: 2x leveraged ETFs on SPCX did $4B (long) and $2.56B (short) in first-week volume; CNBC called the 2x long "the riskiest SpaceX stock trade of all" — [CNBC 2026-06-19](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/19/spacex-stock-risk-buy-sell.html)
- SPCX realised path: from ~$150 open (2026-06-12) to a $104.83 low (2026-08-03) — a ~30% drawdown — then $162.57 (2026-10-09), +55% off the low (my calculation); Zoombangla reported a 58% rebound from the August low — [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/4025374/spacex-stock-just-crashed-below-its-ipo-price-heres-the-bull-case-nobody-can-ignore); [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/spcx-stock-162-57-spacex-8-billion-bull-292-bear-110/); [Zoombangla](https://inews.zoombangla.com/spacex-share-price-rebounds-58-from-august-low-after-ipo-slump)

### Inferences
- The IVOL/MAX literature and the IPO-constraint literature point the same way for the first 180 days of a mega-IPO with a 4% float and retail lottery demand: expect SPCX to underperform into/around lockup expiries and expect its options to be rich. That thesis would have worked from June to early August 2026 (−30%) and failed from August to October (+55% rebound while IV fell to all-time lows), illustrating the "timing" problem the Susquehanna comment captured.
- For a relative options trade today, the literature supports selling the richer-IVOL name's options only when its IV–RV gap is wide (Goyal-Saretto) — SPCX's IV ~50–55 against realised vol that CNBC described as unusually calm since late August is still a positive gap, but much smaller than the 100-point gap of late July; TSLA's IV percentile of 16% (Barchart snapshot) suggests TSLA vol is cheap by its own history, making "long TSLA vol / short SPCX vol" a defensible but modest-edge expression into the Oct 28 TSLA print and the Nov SPCX print, with the final 2026-12-08 unlock as the main SPCX tail.
- Direction for a cash pair: the lottery-stock premium argues for short-SPCX/long-TSLA in expectation, but with 0.66 correlation and both names rated overvalued, the edge is in SPCX-specific supply events, not in the factor.
