## Key Question 1 — With only ~4 months of SPCX history (listed 2026-06-12), what do risk-model vendors and quant practitioners do with a new listing, and when does it become eligible for standard stat-arb / factor universes?

### Takeaway
Commercial risk models do not wait for history: MSCI and Axioma assign industry/fundamental exposures from day one from observable characteristics, fill price-based exposures (momentum, volatility) with market or peer proxies, and shrink stock-specific risk toward similarly sized sector peers; MSCI reports that for recent IPOs (CoreWeave, Figma) forecasts converged within ~120 trading days, and Axioma (per SimCorp) says the move to fully standard treatment takes "several months to about a year". Practitioner stat-arb universes, by contrast, commonly impose a one-year (252-trading-day) seasoning rule or at least a full regression-estimation window plus rolling size/liquidity screens, so SPCX (about 85 trading days old on 2026-10-13) sits inside the risk models' estimation universes but would still be excluded from most conventional stat-arb universes, and from the S&P 500 until at least June 2027.

### Cited Findings
- MSCI (June 2026 "Quick Take", "New Listing, No History: Estimating the Risk of an IPO"): a fundamental cross-sectional model "derives exposures from observable characteristics rather than regressions on the stock's own returns", so a new listing "gets industry and fundamental exposures right away, while price-based items such as momentum and volatility need a workaround" — [MSCI](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/quick-take/new-listing-no-history-estimating-the-risk-of-an-ipo) (as summarised by search; page could not be fetched)
- Same MSCI piece: the current MSCI Global Equity Factor Model "estimates specific risk from similarly-sized peers and the stock's own short return window. Momentum is filled with market returns for a new listing, and the stock opens neutral, and live returns enter within two weeks"; "within 120 days, as return history accumulated, the forecasts converged" for recent listings — [MSCI](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/quick-take/new-listing-no-history-estimating-the-risk-of-an-ipo)
- A second search pass summarised the same MSCI piece as: "fills missing momentum history with market returns, initializes residual volatility near the sector 85th percentile, and says exposures converged within 120 days for recent IPOs like CoreWeave and Figma" — [MSCI](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/quick-take/new-listing-no-history-estimating-the-risk-of-an-ipo). The "sector 85th percentile" initialisation appeared in only one of the two summaries, so treat it as plausible but unverified.
- MSCI's older Barra USE4 report on the Facebook IPO (2012) states that "the USE4 Model estimates factor exposures and specific risk of stocks before and after their IPO, and it provides risk estimates which can be used to create proxy assets in Barra Aegis or Barra Portfolio Manager" — [MSCI, "US Market Report – Should I 'Like' Facebook's IPO?"](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/paper/us-market-report-should-i-like-facebook-ipo)
- Axioma (SimCorp) Equity Risk Monitor, week ended 2026-06-12, as summarised in one search pass: for a new listing Axioma "estimates risk from the average profile of similar companies in the same sector and size group, and shrinks market-based factor exposures toward the sector and region cap-weighted mean"; "as trading and fundamental data build up, the model shifts from peer-based estimates to the stock's observed behavior. Stock-specific risk follows the same pattern. The full move to standard coverage typically takes several months to about a year"; the stock "enters Axioma's estimation universe in about a week for short-horizon models and about a month for medium-horizon models" — [SimCorp/Axioma Equity Risk Monitor, week ended 2026-06-12](https://www.simcorp.com/resources/market-commentary/equity-risk-monitors/2026/Equity-Risk-Monitor-Highlights---Week-Ended-2026-06-12). A second search pass on the same URL only surfaced the index-inclusion content; [2nd pass] a third, independent search summary of the same page confirmed the substance: Axioma "first estimates the new stock's factor exposures from peers in the same sector and size group, then shrinks those market-based exposures toward the cap-weighted sector and regional mean", stock-specific risk gets "the same treatment", the move to standard coverage "usually takes several months to about a year, depending on the factor", and the stock enters the estimation universe of short-horizon models "about a week after listing" and medium-horizon models "about a month after". ### Inferences
- For a TSLA/SPCX pair, the practical implication is that any factor-neutralisation that relies on SPCX's own regression betas (market, momentum, volatility) is, as of October 2026, mostly vendor prior plus ~4 months of data; the vendor models themselves say convergence takes ~120 trading days (MSCI) to "several months to a year" (Axioma), so SPCX's betas are only now (mid-October 2026, ~85 trading days) approaching stability.
- Because SPCX's momentum exposure was initialised at market/neutral and residual volatility at a high peer percentile, a naive "factor-neutral" SPCX leg built from vendor exposures in June–September 2026 would have under-hedged momentum and over-estimated idiosyncratic risk; hedge ratios computed then should be re-estimated now.
- Conventional academic stat-arb universes (one-year seasoning) would admit SPCX only from ~June 2027, which coincides with S&P 500 eligibility; this creates a plausible step-change in systematic participation (both passive and stat-arb) around mid-2027 rather than now.

## Key Question 2 — Factor-neutral relative-value construction and the residual-momentum / residual-reversal literature (Blitz-Huij-Martens 2011; Blitz-Huij-Lansdorp-Verbeek 2013; Da-Liu-Schaumburg 2014; Lehmann 1990; Jegadeesh 1990)

### Takeaway
The academic "residual" literature is the closest public analogue to how quant desks neutralise a pair: regress each stock on market/size/value (and often momentum and industry), trade the residual rather than the raw return, and scale by residual volatility. Doing so roughly doubles risk-adjusted returns for both momentum (gross Sharpe 0.90 vs 0.45) and one-month reversal (2× to 3× conventional), removes the time-varying factor exposures that make raw momentum/reversal crash-prone, and — unlike raw short-term reversal — survives realistic costs in large caps. Only the residual (non-fundamental) component of one-month reversal is profitable; the part driven by cash-flow news does not revert.

### Cited Findings
- Blitz, Huij & Martens, "Residual Momentum", Journal of Empirical Finance 18(3), 506–521 (2011): residual momentum "earns risk-adjusted profits that are about twice as large as those associated with total return momentum; is more consistent over time; and less concentrated in the extremes of the cross-section of stocks" — [EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/22252)
- Annualised gross Sharpe ratio "for residual (total) return momentum is 0.90 (0.45)"; residuals are the error term from a regression of total returns on the three Fama-French factors over the past 36 months, ranked on residual return scaled by its 12-month standard deviation — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/stripping-risks-from-a-stock-momentum-strategy/) (secondary; the paper's own tables were not accessible)
- Huij & Lansdorp (2017) revisit the result across regions and later periods and find residual momentum "has significantly higher return-to-risk ratios than conventional momentum" and that the result "holds up across global universes and out-of-sample periods" — [EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/100801)
- Blitz, Huij, Lansdorp & Verbeek, "Short-term residual reversal", Journal of Financial Markets 16(3), 477–504 (2013): conventional reversal strategies "carry dynamic exposures to Fama-French factors" which the residual approach avoids; residual reversal "earns risk-adjusted returns that are twice as large as those of a conventional reversal strategy"; "residual reversal strategies generate statistically and economically significant profits net of trading costs, even when we restrict our sample to large-cap stocks over the post-1990 period" — [EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/40707); [Robeco summary (Nov 2011)](https://www.robeco.com/en-int/insights/2011/11/short-term-residual-reversal)
- Da, Liu & Schaumburg, "A Closer Look at the Short-Term Return Reversal" (NY Fed Staff Report 513, 2011; Management Science 2014): the standard one-month reversal profit decomposes into four components and "only the residual component... is significant and positive in the data"; a residual-based strategy "generates a highly significant risk-adjusted return three times the size of the standard reversal strategy" over 1982–2009; the Management Science version adds that "a simple enhanced reversal strategy generates a risk-adjusted return four times the size of the standard reversal strategy" using analyst forecast revisions to measure cash-flow news — [NY Fed SR 513](https://newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr513.html); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fip:fednsr:513)
- Same paper: the sample covers "relatively large and liquid stocks accounting for about 75% of U.S. equity market capitalization over the period January 1982 through March 2009"; the residual-sorted strategy "stays economically significant after a conservatively estimated monthly trading friction of 0.80% (1.57% (1.34%) when applied to stocks sorted intra-industry based on prior-month discount rate/liquidity news)"; mechanisms are "liquidity shocks on the long side, and investor sentiment on the short side, where short-sale constraints prevent the immediate elimination of overvaluation" — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/animal-spirits/refined-short-term-reversal-strategies); [NY Fed SR 513](https://newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr513.html)
- Lehmann (1990), weekly horizon, as summarised by Alpha Architect: prior-week winners fell the next week by "roughly 0.35% to 0.55%" while prior-week losers gained "roughly 0.86% to 1.24% per week"; contrarian strategies "produced abnormal returns of over 2% per month" — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/quantitative-momentum-research-short-term-return-reversal/) (secondary; original tables not checked)
- Jegadeesh (1990), "Evidence of Predictable Behavior of Security Returns", Journal of Finance 45(3), 881–898, documents the monthly-horizon reversal that is the reason "academics commonly use 2-12 momentum" — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/blog/2015/01/14/quantitative-momentum-research-short-term-return-reversal/)
- Asymmetry relevant to a long/short pair: "Asymmetric Reversals" (NBER w35840, Baldi-Lanfranchi, Collin-Dufresne et al.) finds that "positive residual shocks reverse much more slowly than negative shocks" — [NBER w35840](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35840)
- Antón & Polk's "Connected Stocks" (JF 2014) shows that once factor exposures, style and sector are controlled for, pairs with common active-fund owners still have excess correlation, and a "cross-stock reversal strategy built on these connections reportedly earns abnormal returns of more than 9% per year after controlling for standard factors" — [Antón & Polk, LSE](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/ConnectedStocks.pdf) (see Section 5 for detail)

### Inferences
- The standard desk recipe implied by this literature for a TSLA/SPCX pair would be: (i) estimate each leg's betas to market, size, value, momentum (and the Nasdaq-100 / "AI-space complex" if desired) — for SPCX this must use high-frequency (daily) data over the ~4 months available or vendor priors; (ii) form the residual spread; (iii) scale by residual volatility; (iv) trade residual reversal on a 1–4 week horizon and/or residual momentum on a 6–12 month horizon. The literature's Sharpe gains come from step (iii) as much as from (i)–(ii).
- Da-Liu-Schaumburg's decomposition is directly relevant to the "Musk post" question: a joint TSLA/SPCX move that is non-fundamental (no cash-flow news, no analyst revision) is in the component that reverts; a move accompanied by a Starship milestone, a Q3 print or a merger announcement is in the cash-flow component that does not.
- The NBER asymmetry result suggests the short leg of a pair after a joint upward retail spike (short the leg that over-reacted upward) is slower to pay off than the long leg after a joint downward spike.

### Gaps
- Primary journal tables of Blitz et al. (2011, 2013) and Da et al. (2014) were not accessible; Sharpe figures are from CXO Advisory / Robeco summaries.
- Found no publication from a bank quant desk (JPM QDS, DB, SocGen, GS QIS, MS QDS, BofA, Barclays QPS, Nomura) that describes its pair-neutralisation recipe or a "retail/meme" factor; none was surfaced by press search in 2024–2026.
- No paper found that specifically defines or neutralises a "retail/meme" factor in a long/short pair; the closest proxies are Robinhood-herding measures (Section 3) and the Welch "Robinhood crowd" portfolio.

## Key Question 3 — What is the evidence that retail attention shocks (Musk posts, Robinhood herding, Google search spikes) move TSLA and related names together, and does the co-movement mean-revert (tradable) or persist?

### Takeaway
The attention literature is consistent that retail attention shocks produce short-run buying pressure followed by reversal: Robinhood herding episodes are followed by average 5-day abnormal returns of −3% (−6% for extreme herding) and about −5% over the following month (−9% for the most extreme events); Google-search spikes predict higher prices for about two weeks and reversal within a year; Musk's crypto tweets produced significant positive abnormal returns and volume only for Dogecoin (not Bitcoin) over a 47-event sample. Direct evidence that Musk posts move TSLA is mixed — a 2025 journal study of 3,158 tweets / 33 events finds no significant effect, while several student theses find event-day effects, especially for negative-sentiment posts. For TSLA/SPCX specifically, the only co-movement statistic found is a CNBC-cited 30-day correlation of 0.66 (early October 2026), with joint rallies documented on 2026-09-03 (SPCX +6.4%, TSLA +5.4%) and on a Monday in early October 2026 (SPCX +7.6%, TSLA +2.2%); no study yet tests whether that co-movement reverts.

### Cited Findings

Retail attention → buying pressure → reversal (general evidence)
- Barber & Odean, "All That Glitters", RFS 21(2), 785–818 (2008): "individual investors are net buyers of attention-grabbing stocks" (stocks in the news, with unusually high volume, or extreme one-day returns); the mechanism is search cost, since "attention-driven buying results from the difficulty that investors have searching the thousands of stocks they can potentially buy, while individual investors do not face the same search problem when selling"; data span 1991–1999 across four datasets; individuals made "nearly twice as many purchases as sales of stocks experiencing unusually high trading volume" and were net buyers of both the previous day's big winners and big losers; institutional buying is least influenced by attention — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v21y2008i2p785-818.html); [paper PDF](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/allthatglitters_rfs_2008.pdf); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/use-the-cone-of-silence-when-buying-stocks/)
- Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, "Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users" (working paper first dated 2020-10-18, revised July 2021; published JF 2022): "The top stocks bought each day show average five-day abnormal returns of -3% (-6%) for more extreme herding"; a UC Davis summary says the top-bought stocks "fall about five percent over the next month, with the most extreme herding events seeing reversals of nine percent"; the Robinhood app directs attention "to the same small subset of stocks, such as the 20 'Top Movers'"; "half of users are first-time investors"; the effect is "more pronounced in the post-Covid period"; the authors argue the negative returns "are not driven by the bid-ask spread" — [SSRN working paper PDF](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/SSRN-id3715077.pdf); [UC Davis GSM](https://gsm.ucdavis.edu/news/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market); [Evidence Investor / Swedroe](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/post/why-most-robinhood-traders-earn-lousy-returns). Note the two UC Davis summaries describe horizons differently (5-day vs 1-month).
- Welch, "The Wisdom of the Robinhood Crowd", Journal of Finance 77(3), 1489–1527 (2022; NBER w27866, 2020): Robinhood investors "added to their holdings during the March 2020 COVID bear market"; the consensus portfolio "tilted primarily toward stocks with high past share volume and dollar-trading volume (themselves mostly big stocks)"; "from mid-2018 to mid-2020, an aggregated crowd consensus portfolio... had both good timing and good alpha"; a Robinhood-like portfolio back-tested to 1980 "did not underperform" — [RePEc JF listing](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/bla/jfinan/v77y2022i3p1489-1527.html); [NBER w27866](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27866); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/robinhood)
- Da, Engelberg & Gao, "In Search of Attention", Journal of Finance (2011): Google SVI for Russell 3000 stocks 2004–2008 "reflects retail investor attention more than other measures"; rising SVI "predicts higher prices over the next couple of weeks and a later price reversal within the year"; stocks with large pre-IPO search increases "outperformed others by about 6% on the first day" and "showed significant long-run reversals"; results are read as "direct support for Barber and Odean's (2008) price-pressure hypothesis" — [paper PDF, UCSD Rady](https://rady.ucsd.edu/faculty/directory/engelberg/pub/portfolios/GOOGLE.pdf); [NBER conference version](https://projects.nber.org/confer/2009/mms09/Da_Engelberg_Gao.pdf)
- Pedersen, "Game On: Social Networks and Markets" (Journal of Financial Economics, 2022): a model with naive investors who learn from peers, "fanatics" who may spread false information, and rational short- and long-term traders; generates "bubbles, excess volatility, bursts of high volume, price momentum, and reversal in securities markets"; GameStop (early 2021) is the main application — [AQR working paper page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Social-Networks-and-Markets-GameStop-and-Beyond); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/social-networks-and-markets/)
- Cookson & Niessner, "Why Don't We Agree?", Journal of Finance 75(1), 173–228 (2020): StockTwits-based disagreement is "evenly split between" information differences and interpretation differences, "but within-group disagreement is more tightly related to trading volume than cross-group disagreement"; a follow-up finds "daily disagreement and trading volume are strongly related to one another both in the original sample and out of sample through 2021" — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v75y2020i1p173-228.html); [SSRN 4529594](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4529594)

Musk-specific co-movement
- Ante, "How Elon Musk's Twitter activity moves cryptocurrency markets", Technological Forecasting and Social Change 186 (2023): "Based on a sample of 47 crypto-related Twitter events, significant positive abnormal returns and trading volumes were found following such events. Price effects are only significant on average for Dogecoin-related tweets, but not for Bitcoin"; the Bitcoin null is attributed to "significant price effects of positive and negative news cancelling each other out" — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/tefoso/v186y2023ipas0040162522006333.html). ## Key Question 4 — What realistic Sharpe ratios and capacity do quant-desk and academic sources attribute to residual-reversal / short-term reversal in US mega-caps after costs, 2015–2026?

### Takeaway
No source found gives a clean after-cost Sharpe for mega-cap short-term reversal over exactly 2015–2026. The best available evidence: (a) residual reversal is profitable net of costs in large caps post-1990 (Blitz et al. 2013) and the residual component of monthly reversal survives a 0.80%/month friction assumption over 1982–2009 (Da et al.); (b) restricting to the largest US stocks still yields "up to 30 basis points per week" of reversal profit (de Groot, Huij & Zhou 2012); (c) Robeco's 2023 FAJ study over MSCI World 1985–2021 finds individual short-term signals have >6% p.a. gross six-factor alpha but net alpha "less than −2%" at 25 bp per single trip, with costs still eroding "more than two-thirds" of a composite's return unless turnover-reducing buy/hold rules are used; (d) one NBER paper puts the capacity ceiling for short-term reversal at ~$9bn in the US / $13bn globally; (e) Novy-Marx & Velikov (2016) find few anomalies with >50% monthly turnover survive costs. Retail-backtest services report gross Sharpe ~0.84 for monthly US reversal, with high cost sensitivity.

### Cited Findings
- Blitz, Huij, Lansdorp & Verbeek (JFM 2013): residual reversal "generate[s] statistically and economically significant profits net of trading costs, even when we restrict our sample to large-cap stocks over the post-1990 period" — [EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/40707)
- Da, Liu & Schaumburg: residual reversal remains economically significant "after a conservatively estimated monthly trading friction of 0.80%" on a universe of "relatively large and liquid stocks accounting for about 75% of U.S. equity market capitalization", January 1982–March 2009 — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/animal-spirits/refined-short-term-reversal-strategies); [NY Fed SR 513](https://newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr513.html)
- de Groot, Huij & Zhou, "Another Look at Trading Costs and Short-Term Reversal Profits" (Journal of Banking & Finance 2012): "the impact of trading costs on the strategies' profitability can largely be attributed to excessively trading in small cap stocks. Limiting the stock universe to large cap stocks significantly reduces trading costs"; "significant reversal profits up to 30 basis points per week when focusing on the largest U.S. stocks"; a search summary also attributed "net returns of roughly 30 to 50 basis points per week" to the same work — [ERIM event page](https://www.erim.eur.nl/research/events/detail/1992/); [EFMA 2011 paper](https://efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2011-Braga/papers/0259_update.pdf)
- Blitz, Hanauer, Honarvar, Huisman & van Vliet, "Beyond Fama-French Factors: Alpha from Short-Term Signals", Financial Analysts Journal 79(4), 96–117 (2023), sample MSCI World standard index December 1985–December 2021: individual short-term signals (reversal, short-term momentum, analyst revisions, short-term risk, seasonality) "had an average gross six-factor alpha of more than 6% per annum", but turnover "pushed net alpha to less than -2% when considering realistic transaction costs of 25 bps per single trip"; for the composite "transaction costs still eroded more than two-thirds from the net return"; "buy 10/hold 50" rules kept more alpha after costs — [CFA Institute FAJ](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2023/beyond-fama-french-factors-alpha-from-short-term-signals); [Robeco summary](https://www.robeco.com/en-au/insights/2022/05/beyond-fama-french-alpha-from-short-term-signals); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/alpha-from-short-term-signals/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v79y2023i4p96-117.html)
- Capacity: "short-term reversal strategies do not survive transactions costs at sizes greater than $9 billion in the U.S. or $13 billion globally" (NBER conference paper; authors not identified in the search summary) — [NBER conference paper f68262](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f68262/f68262.pdf)
- Novy-Marx & Velikov, "A Taxonomy of Anomalies and Their Trading Costs", RFS 29(1), 104–147 (2016): "most anomalies with less than 50% turnover per month generate significant net spreads when designed to mitigate transaction costs; few with higher turnover do"; "strategies based on size, value, and profitability have the greatest capacity to support new capital" — [NBER w20721](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20721); [RFS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v29y2016i1p104-147..html)
- [2nd pass] Nagel, "Evaporating Liquidity" (RFS 2012; NBER w17653, 2011): short-term reversal returns are a proxy for the return to liquidity provision; they "vary a great deal over time and can be forecast using the VIX"; expected returns and conditional Sharpe ratios "rise sharply when the VIX is high", as in 2007–09; even industry-portfolio reversal, which earns little on average, posts high returns when VIX is high — [NBER w17653](https://nber.org/papers/w17653). A Quantpedia post reports a 46% raw correlation between large-stock reversal returns and VIX and about 5.37 bp extra next-five-day reversal return per VIX point (R² 2.18%) — [Quantpedia (secondary, unverified)](https://vvv.quantpedia.com/es/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/808)
- Medhat & Schmeling, "Short-term Momentum" (RFS 35(3), 1480–1526, 2022; DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhab055; sample US common nonfinancial shares July 1963–December 2018, value-weighted, monthly rebalancing, FF5+momentum benchmark) [2nd pass: citation confirmed via [City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/31278/) and [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/short-term-momentum/)]: double-sorting on last month's return and share turnover "reveals significant short-term reversal among low-turnover stocks whereas high-turnover stocks exhibit short-term momentum"; the latter "survives transaction costs and is strongest among the largest, most liquid, and most extensively covered stocks" — summarised in search results alongside [CEPR DP15857](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP15857.pdf) (the DP link's correspondence to this paper is unverified)
- Retail backtest reference: Quantpedia's monthly US short-term reversal strategy reports "Sharpe ratio 0.84 and Beta of 0.15" (gross; period and cost assumptions not stated) and cautions that weekly/biweekly rebalancing "implies high transaction costs and slippage" — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-in-stocks)
- Residual momentum (longer horizon) gross Sharpe 0.90 vs 0.45 conventional, and Huij & Lansdorp (2017) out-of-sample confirmation — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/stripping-risks-from-a-stock-momentum-strategy/); [EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/100801)

### Inferences
- Combining these sources: for a two-name mega-cap pair the capacity constraint ($9bn aggregate for a diversified US reversal book) is not binding, but the cost constraint is — a single pair rebalanced weekly would face the same ~25 bp single-trip assumption the Robeco study uses, so the Robeco "buy 10 / hold 50" style hysteresis (enter on a strong signal, exit only when the signal decays well past neutral) is the relevant design lesson.
- Since both TSLA and SPCX are high-turnover names, the Medhat–Schmeling result (high-turnover stocks show short-term momentum, not reversal) is a direct caution against a mechanical one-month reversal rule on either leg; the reversal evidence that does survive in large caps is residual (factor-stripped) and short-horizon (weekly), not raw monthly.
- Nagel (2012) implies that the expected payoff of a TSLA/SPCX residual-reversal trade is state-dependent: it should be sized up when VIX (and dealer/market-maker balance-sheet stress) is high and is likely thin in calm regimes such as SPCX's late-September/October 2026 implied-vol normalisation.
- No source gives a 2015–2026 after-cost Sharpe; a prudent synthesis would be that gross Sharpe of ~0.8–0.9 for diversified residual reversal/momentum books shrinks to materially less after 25 bp costs, and that the figure for a single pair with ~4 months of history on one leg cannot be grounded in any published number.

### Gaps
- No bank-desk publication (e.g., JPM QDS / DB / MS QDS reversal baskets) with after-cost Sharpe for 2015–2026 was surfaced.
- The NBER capacity paper's authors and sample period were not identified in the search summary; the figures should be verified against the PDF.
- No study found that reports residual-reversal performance specifically for the Magnificent-7-type mega-caps or for 2020–2026, the period of elevated retail participation. [2nd pass] A targeted search for 2020s large-cap reversal performance returned only 2020–2021 factor-volatility commentary (e.g., [Invesco IQS](https://invesco.com/ch/en/insights/IQS-factors-2020-21-looking-back-and-ahead.html)) and an S&P 500 weekly-reversal ETF launch (Vesper, 2021, [ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/smart-beta-channel/vesper-debuts-a-dynamic-etf-that-buys-on-the-dip/)); no post-2015 after-cost Sharpe was found.

## Key Question 6 — Lead–lag and "connected stocks" return predictability via common institutional ownership, common analysts, supply-chain links and common news (Antón & Polk 2014; Cohen & Frazzini 2008; Menzly & Ozbas 2010; Hou 2007; Lo & MacKinlay 1990)

### Takeaway
The connected-stocks literature provides the academic basis for treating TSLA and SPCX as linked beyond fundamentals: common active ownership forecasts excess return correlation and supports a cross-stock reversal strategy earning >9%/year abnormal returns (Antón & Polk), while economic links (customer–supplier, cross-industry) generate lead–lag predictability of 100–150 bp/month in the original samples, attenuated after publication. TSLA and SPCX are linked by common CEO/controlling shareholder, common retail holder base, a direct equity stake (Tesla's ~19M SPCX shares), and — per Q2 2026 13Fs — common hedge-fund ownership, so all three channels are present. [2nd pass] A direct customer–supplier link also exists but is small: Tesla booked ~$573M of 2025 revenue from xAI (Megapacks, $430M) and SpaceX (mainly vehicles, $143.3M), ≈0.6% of Tesla's ~$97.7bn revenue; and Ali & Hirshleifer (2020) find customer/supplier momentum is subsumed by shared-analyst-coverage momentum (1.68%/month alpha), pointing to common coverage/ownership rather than the supply contract as the operative channel.

### Cited Findings
- Antón & Polk, "Connected Stocks", Journal of Finance (2014): "The degree of shared ownership forecasts cross-sectional variation in return correlation" after controlling for factor exposures, style, sector and other pair traits; the 2003 mutual fund trading scandal is used as a natural experiment showing shared ownership "itself drives the excess comovement"; the effect "is stronger for small stocks and when common owners see heavy inflows or outflows"; "a cross-stock reversal strategy built on these connections... generates significant abnormal returns of more than 9% per year"; "long-short hedge fund index returns covary negatively with this strategy, suggesting these funds may exacerbate this excess comovement" — [Antón & Polk, LSE PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/ConnectedStocks.pdf); [FMG working paper (2010)](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-08/connected-stocks.pdf); [IESE Insight summary](https://www.iese.edu/insight/articles/connect-stocks-improve-trading)
- Cohen & Frazzini, "Economic Links and Predictable Returns", Journal of Finance 63(4), 1977–2011 (2008): "A long-short equity strategy based on this effect yields monthly alphas of over 150 basis points"; the hypothesis is that "with investors subject to attention constraints, stock prices do not promptly incorporate news about economically related firms"; results are "largely unaffected in both magnitude and significance by controlling for the three-factor model, liquidity, own-firm momentum, industry momentum, within-industry lead-lag relationships, and across-industry momentum" — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v63y2008i4p1977-2011.html); [AQR summary](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Economic-Links-and-Predictable-Returns)

- Menzly & Ozbas, "Market Segmentation and Cross-predictability of Returns", Journal of Finance 65(4), 1555–1580 (2010): "stocks in economically related customer-supplier industries cross-predict returns of each other" — [vLex summary](https://international.vlex.com/vid/can-economic-links-explain-1042472695); [University of Vaasa thesis citing it](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/bitstreams/c7c8caca-a33c-40db-ba59-24cf10ca021c/download)

### Inferences
- Antón & Polk's mechanism (flow-driven contagion through common active owners, strongest when those owners face in/outflows) maps directly onto the SPCX lock-up calendar: insiders and pre-IPO funds (D1, Darsana, Atreides are highly concentrated) that sell SPCX into unlocks do not create TSLA selling, but concentrated hedge funds that hold both and face redemptions would; the connected-stocks reversal strategy says that such flow-induced co-movement is the component that reverts.
- [2nd pass] The customer–supplier link is real but small relative to Tesla (≈0.6% of 2025 revenue from SpaceX+xAI combined) and is mostly an xAI→Tesla Energy flow; Cohen–Frazzini-style predictability runs from customer news to supplier returns, i.e., SPCX (customer, incl. xAI capex) news → TSLA (supplier). Given Ali & Hirshleifer's result that customer/supplier momentum is subsumed by shared-analyst-coverage momentum, and that the two stocks will share most sell-side and retail coverage, the more relevant 'connection' measure for this pair is shared coverage / common owners, not the supply contract.
- Cohen–Frazzini's attention-constraint channel suggests that news about SpaceX fundamentals (launch cadence, Starlink revenue) might be incorporated into TSLA with a lag via Tesla's ~19M-share stake and the merger probability; the post-publication attenuation found by Pinchuk warns that any such lag in two of the most-covered stocks in the world is likely to be short and small.
